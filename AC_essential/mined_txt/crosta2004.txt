ABSTRACT: 
A Modern Analog Technique (MAT5201/31) has been applied to fossil 
diatom assemblages to provide down-core estimates of February sea-
surface temperatures (SSTs) and of sea ice duration over the past 
220,000 years at 56°40'S, 160°14'E.  At the core location, sea ice 
progression lagged the SST drop by ~1 ka at interglacial-glacial 
transitions, and sea ice retreat was almost synchronous to the SST 
increase at glacial-interglacial terminations.  Sea ice increased 
continuously during glacial periods to reach its maximum extent 
at the end of glacial times, although SSTs were almost constant 
during glacials.  This indicates that SSTs are the major parameter 
determining the advance and retreat of sea ice at transitions, 
but that the sea ice advance during glacial conditions may be 
related to positive feedbacks of the ice on albedo, air temperature 
and meridional wind stress.  The strong correlation (r=0.75) between 
sea ice duration at the core location and the Vostok CO2 record 
argues for a control of Antarctic sea ice extent on atmospheric 
CO2 concentration via the modification of the ocean-to-atmosphere 
gas balance.

DESCRIPTION:    
Diatom-based SSTs and Sea Ice Extent for the Indian sector of the 
Southern Ocean. February SSTs and sea ice cover were estimated 
by applying an evolution (MAT5 201/31, Crosta et al. 2004) of the 
Modern Analog Technique described by Crosta et al. 1998 (MAT5 195/33)
to fossil diatom assemblages.  Diatom taxa are divided into 4 groups
by similar environmental preference: SIZ (Sea Ice Zone) Group,  
POOZ (Permanent Open Ocean Zone) Group, Strat (Water Stratification) 
Group, and SAZ (Subantarctic Zone) Group.  Individual taxa contained 
in each group can be found in Appendix Table 2 of Crosta et al. 2004.

Core location:
Gravity core SO136-111, Emerald Basin, East Indian sector, 
Southern Ocean: 56°40'S, 160°14'E, water depth 3912 m.
