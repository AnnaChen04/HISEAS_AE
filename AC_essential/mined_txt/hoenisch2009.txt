ABSTRACT:
The dominant period of Pleistocene glacial cycles changed during 
the mid-Pleistocene from 40,000 years to 100,000 years, for as yet 
unknown reasons.  Here we present a 2.1-million-year record of sea 
surface partial pressure of CO2 (PCO2), based on boron isotopes 
in planktic foraminifer shells, which suggests that the atmospheric 
partial pressure of CO2 (pCO2) was relatively stable before the 
mid-Pleistocene climate transition.  Glacial PCO2 was ~31 
microatmospheres higher before the transition (more than 1 million 
years ago), but interglacial PCO2 was similar to that of late 
Pleistocene interglacial cycles (<450,000 years ago). These 
estimates are consistent with a close linkage between atmospheric 
CO2 concentration and global climate, but the lack of a gradual 
decrease in interglacial PCO2 does not support the suggestion 
that a long-term drawdown of atmospheric CO2 was the main cause 
of the climate transition.

DESCRIPTION: 
Stable isotope and d11B data from core ODP 668B over the 
Mid-Pleistocene Transition.  We constructed a high-resolution 
oxygen isotope (d18O) record from shells of the surface dwelling 
Globigerinoides ruber (250-300 µm).  The record allows us to 
establish an age model, which is simultaneously tied to the stack 
of 57 globally distributed benthic d18O records (LR04 stack) 
and the planktic d18O record of ODP 677.  Some intervals were 
measured in duplicate, all individual d18O and d13C data are 
provided and average d18O data for duplicate measurements. 
Data were analysed by Monika Segl at Bremen University, 
using a Finnigan MAT 252 and a Kiel device (type "Bremen").

Boron isotope data were then converted into pH estimates, 
using an empirical calibration for G. sacculifer 
(Sanyal et al. 2001).  PCO2 estimates were then made 
for constant or variable ocean alkalinity scenarios 
(see Supplementary Online Material for Hönisch et al. 2009).  

ODP 668B: 4°46.23'N, 20°55.62'W, 2693m depth
