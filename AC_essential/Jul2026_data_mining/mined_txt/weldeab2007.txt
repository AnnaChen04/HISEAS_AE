ABSTRACT:
A detailed reconstruction of West African monsoon hydrology over the 
past 155,000 years suggests a close linkage to northern high-latitude 
climate oscillations. Ba/Ca ratio and oxygen isotope composition of 
planktonic foraminifera in a marine sediment core from the Gulf of 
Guinea, in the eastern equatorial Atlantic (EEA), reveal centennial-
scale variations of riverine freshwater input that are synchronous 
with northern high-latitude stadials and interstadials of the 
penultimate interglacial and the last deglaciation. EEA Mg/Ca-based 
sea surface temperatures (SSTs) were decoupled from northern high- 
latitude millennial-scale fluctuation and primarily responded to 
changes in atmospheric greenhouse gases and low-latitude solar 
insolation. The onset of enhanced monsoon precipitation lags behind 
the changes in EEA SSTs by up to 7000 years during glacial-interglacial 
transitions. This study demonstrates that the stadial-interstadial 
and deglacial climate instability of the northern high latitudes 
exerts dominant control on the West African monsoon dynamics through 
an atmospheric linkage.

DESCRIPTION: 
A continuous marine sedimentary record of eastern equatorial Atlantic thermal 
and West African monsoon evolution over the past 155,000 years.  

Mg/Ca, Ba/Ca and d18O were measured on Globigerinoides ruber (variety pink) 
with shell size 250-300 micron.  The age model for the MD03-2707 record was 
developed using 24 14C-AMS datings measured at the Lawrence Livermore National 
Laboratory (Livermore, USA) and at the Leibniz-Laboratorium fur Altersbestimmung 
und Isotopenforschung, Universitat Kiel (Kiel, Germany). 
14C ages are converted to calendar age using the software Calib version 5.0.1  
assuming a reservoir age of 400 years. For 14C-dates beyond the calibration range 
of the Calib software, we use the online software Fairbanks0805 calibration curve. 
The final age model for the 14C dated interval is based on two polynomial fits. 
For the age interval between 54,850 and 120,300 years BP, the age model is 
developed by alignment of planktonic foraminiferal d18O in MD03-2707 with 
the de18O of NGRIP ice core. For ages older than 119,500 yr BP, the age model 
is developed by aligning benthic foraminiferal d18O in MD03-2707 with the d18O 
of the stack record LR04 (Lisiecki and Raymo (2005).  For detailed description 
of the methods, please read the Supplementary Online Material for 
Weldeab et al. 2007.
